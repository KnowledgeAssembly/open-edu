Stage 3 — Visual DNA

Illustration Language — Human Figures

How should people appear in OpenEdu illustrations? The style must be built from the same geometric primitive (circle), celebrate diversity with dignity, and never reduce learners to caricatures.

Philosophy: "Built from the same geometric language. Human-centered, celebrating learners with diversity and dignity." Pipili communicates through posture and orientation, not facial expressions.
Option A

Circle People

Figures built from stacked circles and rounded rectangles. Head (circle), body (rounded rect), legs (small rounded rects). Pure geometric assembly. Each person is a composition of the primitive — no lines, no sharp edges.

Assembly of primitives
pure geometry on-brand abstract
Option B

Silhouette Assembly

Overlapping translucent shapes form a human silhouette. A circle head sits atop a rounded torso. The transparency creates depth where shapes overlap. Dignified, minimal, no facial features — posture and proportion convey identity.

Translucent layering
dignified diverse scalable
Option C

Posture Glyphs

Minimalist stick figures with circle heads. Body is a single line, arms and legs are simple strokes. Emotion comes entirely from posture — leaning forward (curious), arms raised (celebrating), relaxed stance (calm). No faces needed.

Posture = emotion
expressive simplest motion-ready
Option D

Gradient Figures

Overlapping circles with vertical gradients — dark at top (head), fading to transparent at bottom. Creates an ethereal, weightless feeling. The gradient suggests energy flowing downward, grounding the figure. Multiple figures in different palettes show diversity.
